# The person splits the reasoning

**Claim:** each claim is one topic the person wrote, with one sentence, a statement and a proof; the splitting into claims is not left to a model.

- **Grade:** convention, untested for the loop; supported with caveat for checking, which small claims help every time practice reports, while help for thinking is reported only where the person did the splitting.
- **Evidence:** [review, Q4](../../evidence/autoformalization/review.md); [Kirov 2026a](../../evidence/bibliography.md), results; [Thoma 2022](../../evidence/bibliography.md) and [Otte 2026](../../evidence/bibliography.md), Table 3, no grade effect in the one controlled comparison; [position, condition 4](../../evidence/autoformalization/position.md).
- **Check:** `claim-shape` (exact): the title, the one-sentence claim, the Shape, Lean, In words and audit lines, and the Statement, Proof and See also parts; `topic` (exact): the summary, and a parent that lists the claim.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Write the claim as one sentence under the title.
- Give the claim a statement, a proof in numbered steps, and a link to what it uses.
- List the claim in its theorem's table, so the tree shows how the claims join.

## Why

- Small claims make checking fast, and a checked claim ends disputes about it (Tao 2025b).
- Practice reports the effect on thinking only where the person did or followed the splitting.

## Limits

- It does not claim that writing as topics helps a person think or learn: no randomised study was found.
- The check reads the form of a claim, not whether the split is a good one.
